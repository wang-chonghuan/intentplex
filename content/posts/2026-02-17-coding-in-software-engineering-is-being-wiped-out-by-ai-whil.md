Coding in software engineering is being wiped out by AI, while data engineering is far from being eliminated—if anything, it’s getting harder. Even AI memory, context management, evaluation, and A/B testing are forms of data engineering. 

The core of software engineering is to compress originally vague requirements into verifiable specifications and boundaries, and then, within a relatively closed system, construct an implementation that is “correct with certainty.” 

The core of data engineering is to operate in an open world where semantics shift, distributions drift, and ground truth is often not directly observable; it uses an evidence pipeline—collection, cleaning, observability, experimentation/offline regression evaluation, monitoring, and replayability—to continuously approximate the objective, and to quickly detect, attribute, and correct when changes occur. In that sense, it’s more about managing uncertainty than eliminating it.
